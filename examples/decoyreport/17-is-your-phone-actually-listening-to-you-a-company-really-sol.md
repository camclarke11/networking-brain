You talk about something, an hour later you see an ad for it. So your phone is listening, right?
In 2024, a media company pitched advertisers a service called Active Listening, ads based on
what people say near their phones. The pitch deck even named Google, Meta, and Amazon as partners.
All three denied it. And in May 2026, the FTC revealed the truth. The service never used voice
data at all. It was old email lists, resold at a markup. Let's x-ray it. Your phone doesn't need
to listen. Your searches, your location, the stories you walked into, and what your friends
looked up say more than any conversation. Then your brain does the rest. You forget the
thousand ads that missed and remember the one that hit. That company sold a fear everyone
already believed. It worked until it cost them $880,000. That's the decoy. Follow for more.
