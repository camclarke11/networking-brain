# The echo

**Format:** F1 Tool
**Tier:** 1 (mid-conversation at an event, with one move and the exact words)
**Situation:** someone at an event is telling you about their business and you're nodding, planning your next question
**A vs B:** plan your next question while they talk → repeat their last few words back as a question, then stop talking
**Length:** 159 words ≈ 55s (at 175 wpm)
**Hook code:** N4 · **Ending code:** E4 · **Named tool:** the echo

---

## TITLE / COVER OPTIONS
1. Keep anyone talking with three words
2. The echo: the laziest way to be a great listener
3. Stop planning your next question

---

## HOOK OPTIONS
**A (used, N4):** The easiest way to keep someone talking is three words long.
**B (N3):** Stop planning your next question while they're talking. Do this instead.
**C (N5):** The thing you do while they're talking is why the conversation dies.

---

## SCRIPT


The easiest way to keep someone talking is three words long.

Dev's telling you about his agency.
You're nodding, planning your next question.
He can tell.

So stop planning and use the echo.
Repeat his last few words back, as a question.
Then stop talking.

He says, we nearly went under last year.
You say, nearly went under?
And he tells you the whole story.

You're thinking, won't he notice I'm copying him?

In 2003, researchers had a waitress repeat some customers' orders back, word for word.
Those tables tipped her more.

It doesn't sound like copying.
It sounds like you're listening.

But most people echo the wrong words.
You skip the boring bit.
You echo the bit with feeling in it.
Went under, not last year.
That's where his real story is.

You'll barely say ten words.
He'll still call you the best chat of the night.

Send this to the friend who talks over everyone at dinner.

## CAPTION

Keep anyone talking with three words.

While they're talking, you're planning your next question, and they can tell. Do this instead.

The echo:
📍 Repeat the last few words they said, as a question.
📍 Then stop talking.
📍 Pick the words with feeling, not the boring ones. "We nearly went under last year" → "Nearly went under?", not "Last year?"
📍 Once or twice per chat is plenty. Every sentence is a parrot.

Try it on three people this week and tell me who told you the best story 👇

| Those tables tipped her more | Same abstract: significantly larger tips when mimicking, and above the pre-experiment baseline | Confirmed |

### ⚠️ Check before posting
- Don't say "doubled". Press coverage claimed it, but the abstract only says "significantly larger". The script says "more".
- The study is about restaurant tips, not conversations. The script only uses it to answer "won't they notice?", which it supports (customers responded well, not suspiciously).
- Repeating someone's last words is a well-known listening technique (negotiators call it mirroring). "The echo" is our own name and our own twist (echo the words with feeling). Don't call it mirroring.
