# 10 — Quality Checklist (the gate every script passes)

Go through every item. **Hard fails** must be fixed before delivering. For **soft checks**, fix what you can and note any trade-off in "Check before posting".

## HARD FAILS

### Topic
- [ ] Tier-checked against `00-what-performs.md`. Tier 3 ideas are moved into a Tier 1 situation or flagged with a Tier 1 alternative.
- [ ] Set in **one specific business moment** a young founder faces (a sales call, a quote, a quiet client, an offer to name, an event, a follow-up), or built on **one famous brand's trick** (Brand X-ray).
- [ ] Tagged with its **pillar** and **Playbook chapter**, and it passes the "my business" test: a 22-year-old with a new agency or a small shop could use it this week.
- [ ] Not a repeat: checked against `content-log.md` (same move, same tool name or same experiment framing = repeat).
- [ ] Original: no tool name, acronym, example, character name or line taken from @theoryofbehaviour or any other creator.

### Hook (it leads, `03-hooks.md` "The lead hook")
- [ ] Line 1 names the situation or the line they say, on its own, in under 15 words.
- [ ] "You" is in the scene within the first three lines, and frame one could show it.
- [ ] The full hook is under 25 words, with no warm-up ("So", "Have you ever", "Did you know") and no citation.
- [ ] One meaning only when read once at speed.
- [ ] **Triple hook:** frame one, the on-screen big text and spoken line 1 point at the same single idea (and the cover title matches). Frame one works on mute (`16-short-form-guide.md` A1, A3).
- [ ] It points forward: an A-vs-B contrast, a withheld person or line, or an outcome that hasn't happened yet, and the ending pays it off.

### Structure
- [ ] One move only. Extra parts went to the Playbook entry.
- [ ] Every beat links to the last with BUT or SO. No "and then", no "also", no spoken lists of tips.
- [ ] The viewer's objection (the Move) or a wrong guess (Brand X-ray) is voiced and answered.
- [ ] At least one re-hook before the 60% mark.
- [ ] **The relay holds** (`04-lesson-engine.md`): the LOOP MAP is filled in, every answer opens the next question, no more than about 4 lines sit outside an open loop, and a turn or pull lands every 2 to 4 lines.
- [ ] **The hook's main question is fully answered in the last 20%**, not the middle, and the premise is restated in one line just before the answer (`16-short-form-guide.md` B5).
- [ ] It ends on the high note: the payoff, then the share prompt, then a hard cut. No outro, no lingering frame (C1).
- [ ] Nothing stops to explain: no moral, no "the lesson is", no named effect. The meaning comes out of what happens.
- [ ] The move is shown with **exact words** or a concrete action, not described in general terms.

### Ending
- [ ] The payoff line closes the hook's loop.
- [ ] The payoff is under 20 words.
- [ ] At most one spoken CTA, after the payoff. Default: a share prompt naming one specific friend.

### Voice
- [ ] "You" is the viewer, in present tense. "I" appears at most twice.
- [ ] One sentence per line; only spoken words in the script section.
- [ ] No banned words or phrases (`02-voice-and-style.md`). No em-dashes or semicolons in the script.
- [ ] Spelling matches `config.json`.

### The Playbook phase
- [ ] While `playbook.status` is `coming_soon`: no mention of the Playbook, a link or the bio in the SCRIPT; the caption carries `playbook.caption_line`. When it's `live`: the last line is `playbook.live_video_cta` and the caption uses `playbook.live_caption_line`.

### Truth and safety
- [ ] **No citation in the spoken script**: no "researchers", "a study", "studies show", "scientists", "psychologists", "according to", no reporter or outlet named as the source. Facts are told as what happened (`07-research-playbook.md` §3).
- [ ] Every fact (and the study behind any mechanism) was checked in a real source this session and is in the fact sheet with a link.
- [ ] No invented numbers or percentages.
- [ ] The move is honest and kind: no manipulation, lying or pressure.

### Length
- [ ] 120 to 170 words (hard ceiling 190). Word count and estimated seconds are in the header.

### ElevenLabs block
- [ ] `## ELEVENLABS` has the SCRIPT's exact words as one paragraph, opens with a tag, and has 7 to 11 tags at the turns, none stacked (`14-voice-tags.md`).

### Storyboard
- [ ] One visual metaphor, and it makes the lesson clearer (someone could explain the lesson by describing what they saw).
- [ ] Every script line is in exactly one shot, in order. Frame one shows the situation in line 1 with the short hook.
- [ ] The cast is the kit's cast. Anything extra a character wears is a costume, listed as a new asset.
- [ ] The library was checked first. Reused assets are under `uses`; new ones are described well enough to build.
- [ ] Big moments are cues pinned to spoken words. Nothing in seconds.
- [ ] Something changes every 2 to 4 seconds, and one shot is marked as the screenshot moment.

## SOFT CHECKS
- [ ] Jagged rhythm: at least two 1 to 3 word lines and at least one line over 12 words.
- [ ] A named character the 3D cast can play.
- [ ] Every shot shows something the words don't already say.
- [ ] A visual progress marker tracks toward the payoff (B8).
- [ ] About a 5th-grade reading level (the checker's estimate is a guide, not a gate).
- [ ] A named tool, if the move is reusable.
- [ ] A paired-contrast line somewhere ("A ends a conversation. B starts one.").
- [ ] The share prompt is specific and a little funny.
- [ ] The caption is a teaser: one line under 125 characters, usually a question about the viewer's business, that names nothing from the video (no brand, story, fact, answer or move) (`05-endings-and-ctas.md`).
- [ ] The payoff calls back to a word or picture from line 1, so the last line flows back into the first on a loop.
- [ ] At least three different lead devices (pointer, wrong guess, turn, escalation, withheld thing, clock, objection).

## The read-aloud test
Read it at speed, out loud, as if to a mate.
- Stumble → shorten.
- Bored → add a BUT or cut.
- **The swipe test:** after each line, ask "do I need the next line?" If the honest answer is no, that line closed a loop without opening one. Add a turn or a pointer, or cut it.
- Cringe at a line you'd tell them to say → rewrite it until you'd actually say it.

## Self-score (in your reasoning, not the output)
Score 1 to 10 on: **Hook, Lead (does every line pull to the next?), Situation, Move, Objection, Ending, Voice.** Anything under 8 gets revised before delivering.
