# 09 — Annotated Breakdowns

Line-by-line teardowns of the reference account's two breakouts, then why its flops flopped. Study the structure; never reuse the content.

## 1. FORD questions — 688k (`examples/theoryofbehaviour/13-…`)

| Line | Beat | What it's doing |
|---|---|---|
| "So, what do you do? / Is why nobody remembers you." | Hook (N2) | Quotes the line every viewer says. Self-recognition in under 2 seconds, plus a sting. |
| "Everyone asks it, so you blend in." | Scene / why | The reason, in 7 words. A *so* link. |
| "Use FORD instead. Four letters and the last two are what make them remember you." | Move + loop | Names the tool. The loop line points at the payoff (the last two letters). |
| "F is family." | Move | One letter per line. Easy to animate as a tool card. |
| "You're thinking, I'm not asking a stranger about their mum. / You don't. / You ask, are you from here originally?" | Objection | Voices the cringe, kills it in two words, gives the exact line. |
| "O is occupation. But skip what do you do. Ask what they're working on right now." | Move + callback | Calls back to the hook. |
| "A job title ends a conversation. / A project starts one." | Mini payoff | Paired contrast, screenshot-able. |
| "But most people stop there. / You don't." | Re-hook | Sets up the second half, exactly where attention dips. |
| "R is recreation… Watch their face change." | Move | A visible reaction, perfect for a character. |
| "Whatever letter lights them up, stay there." | Practical rule | Makes the tool flexible, not robotic. |
| "D is dreams. Where do you want all this to go? / Nobody asks that at events. / So whoever does is who they remember." | Payoff | Closes the loop from line 4: the last letters are why they remember you. |
| "Send this to the friend you always drag to events." | Share CTA | A specific friend, slightly funny. |

**Why it worked:** a universal line in the hook, a tool with four visible steps, an objection, a re-hook at the dip, a loop closed exactly, a share prompt. About 154 words.

## 2. Find the connector — 527k in about 7 hours (`examples/theoryofbehaviour/14-…`)

| Line | Beat | What it's doing |
|---|---|---|
| "When you know nobody at an event, talk to this person first." | Hook (N1) | The dreaded moment plus a secret person. |
| "Skip the speaker and the CEO. / Find the connector." | A vs B | Kills the obvious answer, gives the surprising one. |
| "The guy who knows half the room. / Say his name's Daniel." | Scene | A named character the viewer can picture. |
| "You'll spot him fast. He never stays in one group. And he's always the one who waves the new guy over. Tonight, that's you." | Scene | Visual tells to spot him; puts the viewer in the scene. |
| "But a quick, hey, won't work. He'll forget you by the next group." | Re-hook (BUT) | A problem with the obvious approach. |
| "So give him a job. / I'm starting an agency and want to meet founders. Who should I talk to?" | Move (SO) | Exact words. |
| "You're thinking, why would a guy like that help me? / Because introducing people is his favorite thing. You're just letting him do it." | Objection | Reframes the ask as a gift. |
| "Then he walks you over and says, you two need to meet." | Scene payoff | Shows it working, on screen. |
| "You walked in knowing nobody. / Now every conversation starts with, oh, you're Daniel's friend." | Payoff (E4) | Before → after, closing the hook's "knowing nobody". |
| "Send this to whoever's coming with you." | Share CTA | A natural reason to share right now. |

**Why it worked:** the most universal fear in the niche, a person instead of a theory, a named character, a BUT and a SO, a before → after ending. About 144 words, no study.

## 3. Why the flops flopped

| Post | Views | Problem | What we'd do instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hedonic adaptation | 330 | Abstract feeling (N8), no situation, no move, ends on a tease (E3) | Don't make it. |
| The loudest person in the room | 404 | A claim about other people, not a move for the viewer; garbled audio at the end | F4: "How to tell who actually runs the room in 10 seconds." |
| 300 pennies | 447 | A sales trick with no networking moment; "confusing people" sounds manipulative | Tie it to asking for an intro, or skip. |
| Three steps to make anyone comfortable | 618 | "Anyone" is too broad; three steps feels like a list | F2/F1 at an event: "the person standing alone by the drinks". |
| Halo effect | 784 | Concept-first hook; the study takes the middle; general, not networking | Start in the first 30 seconds at an event; the study is one line. |
| Pushback lines (gain effect) | 3.0k | In the niche, but the move ("disagree first") feels risky and the study does the heavy lifting | Lead with the exact words and the safe version. |
| Your label | 3.2k | Hidden-cause hook (N5) is slow; the move is homework ("ask a friend tonight") | F3 with the exact line to open with. |

**Pattern:** the posts that flopped explain an idea. The posts that won hand the viewer one thing to do in one specific moment.
