# 03 — Hooks

> Examples in quotes come from @theoryofbehaviour unless marked otherwise. They show the shape. **Never reuse their wording.**

A hook has one job: get the viewer to opt in within 1 to 3 seconds. It needs two things (from the Callaway framework Elliott's brain is built on, see `11-storytelling-principles.md`):
1. **Topic clarity:** they know exactly what situation this is about.
2. **On-target curiosity:** they have a specific question, and they believe the answer is worth staying for.

In this niche the hook is usually **one to three short lines (3 to 8 seconds)**:
- **Line 1: the situation or the claim**, in "you" form, about a moment the viewer has lived.
- **Line 2 (optional): the loop**, a promise of one specific unknown ("And the third one sounds backwards.").

---

## The lead hook (do all three, in this order)

A hook doesn't just label the video. It **leads**: it puts the viewer somewhere and points at something they only get by staying. (Why: `11-storytelling-principles.md`.)

1. **Drop in.** "You" plus a moment or object they can see, in the first five words. Present tense, mid-action. *"You're by the drinks, ten minutes in."* / *"Walk into an Apple store."*
2. **Point forward.** Open the loop the payoff will close: an A-vs-B contrast, a withheld person or line ("this person", "one question"), or an outcome that hasn't happened yet ("and it's about to cost you the intro").
3. **Nothing else.** No warm-up ("So,", "Okay", "Have you ever", "Did you know"), no credentials, no "wait till you see this", and never a citation. "In 2009, researchers at UCLA found…" is delay, confusion and disinterest in one line.

Test it: read only the hook. Can you picture the frame? Do you know what the video is about? Is there a question you can't answer yet? Three yeses, or rewrite.

## The hook archetypes (with the reference account's record)

### N1 — Situation + order ⭐ default
*Record: 527k (in about 7 hours).*
Name a moment the viewer dreads, then give a direct instruction that implies a secret.
- "When you know nobody at an event, talk to this person first."
- Ours (business): "When a client goes quiet for a week, don't send 'just checking in'." / "The second you say your price on a call, do this."

**Template:** When [specific uncomfortable moment], [do / say / find] this [person / line / thing] first.
**Why it works:** relevance (I've been there), clarity (it's about events), curiosity (which person?). The answer is concrete enough to show on screen in the next 3 seconds.

### N2 — The line you say is the problem ⭐
*Record: 688k.*
Quote something the viewer says all the time, then blame it.
- "So, what do you do? Is why nobody remembers you."
- Ours (business): "'Let me know if you have any questions' is why nobody books the call." / "'I'm a freelance designer' is why you're the cheap option."

**Template:** "[Thing everyone says]." [Is why / That's why] [bad outcome they've felt].
**Why it works:** instant self-recognition plus a small sting. The fix (what to say instead) is the obvious loop.

### N3 — Contrarian order
*Record: 19k, 5.8k, 3k. Works when the order is about one specific moment; weaker when it's abstract.*
- "Stop doing favors for people you want to impress. Ask them for one instead."
- "Never say, I'm starting an agency, when someone asks what you do."

**Template:** Stop [normal, well-meant behavior]. [Do the opposite] instead.

### N4 — How-to promise with a hard constraint
*Record: 32k, 22k (and 784 when the topic was abstract).*
- "How to get a busy person who doesn't know you to say yes to coffee."
- "How to tell in 10 seconds if someone at an event actually wants to keep talking to you."

**Template:** How to [specific outcome] [with a constraint: a stranger, 10 seconds, no money, even if you're shy].
The constraint is what makes it a hook. "How to network better" is not one.

### N5 — The hidden cause
*Record: 5.2k, 3.2k.*
- "The first thing you say is making the people who could help your business forget you."

**Template:** The [first thing / real reason / one habit] [is causing the bad outcome they already feel].
Weaker on its own. Pair it with a line-2 loop or a concrete scene immediately.

### N6 — Bold claim about people
*Record: 447, 404 (both from the general psychology era).*
- "The loudest person in any room is the least confident."
Use only when the claim is about one specific business moment and you can defend it. Untested in the niche.

### N7 — Steps promise with a twist
*Record: 618 (general psychology era).*
- "Make anyone feel comfortable around you in three steps. And the third one sounds backwards."
The twist line is good. Prefer a specific situation over "anyone".

### N12 — Drop-in: you're already there ⭐
*Record (@zlliott): all of his top 12, 775k, 662k, 264k; median 30.9k across 23 posts.*
Start mid-scene with "you" doing something, then point at what's about to happen.
- Elliott's shape: line 1 a scene ("You're standing inside a post office holding a shotgun."), then the turn that sets the destination.
- Ours: "You're by the drinks, ten minutes in, and every circle's closed. One person in this room can fix that."
- Brand: "Walk into an Apple store and every laptop is open at the same weird angle. Somebody set them like that this morning."

**Template:** You're [in the moment, doing the thing]. [The problem, or the thing that's about to happen]. [Pointer: the person, line or reason they'll only get by staying].
**Why it works:** the viewer is the main character before they've decided to watch, which answers "why should I care?" in two seconds.
Pairs with N1 and N2: an N1 or N2 line works as line 2 or 3 of a drop-in.

### N9, N10, N11 — Brand X-ray hooks (pillar 2)
*Record (@decoyreport): N9 9.75M and 2.6M, median 107k; N10 median 109k; N11 median 57k.*
N9: a brand does something odd, on purpose. N10: same product, one change, a different result. N11: a number paradox. Shapes and rules in `15-brand-xray.md` §3.

### N8 — Abstract feeling
*Record: 330.* Don't use. ("Sometimes the anticipation feels better than getting what you wanted.")

## The loop line (line 2)

When line 1 alone isn't enough, add one line that promises a specific unknown, which the script must pay off:
- "And the third one sounds backwards." → step three is the counterintuitive one.
- "Four letters, and the last two are what make them remember you." → the payoff is D.
- "And it doesn't even mention coffee." → the DM that gets the coffee.
- "There's one question busy people actually answer." → the question.

**Rule:** whatever line 2 promises, the last 20% of the script delivers. If the hook says "the third one sounds backwards", step three must actually surprise.

---

## Contrast: the engine inside every hook

Curiosity comes from the gap between what the viewer does now (A) and what works (B):
- Asking "what do you do?" (A) → nobody remembers you (B).
- Talking to the CEO (A) → talk to the connector (B).
- Doing favors to impress (A) → ask for a favor (B).
- Asking for coffee (A) → ask a question only they can answer (B).

When you plan a script, **write A and B in one line each first**. If B isn't surprising, the idea needs a better angle, not a better hook.

## The 4 hook failures (diagnose before you ship)

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Delay** | The situation isn't clear until line 2 or 3. "Here's something nobody tells you about running a business…" | Put the moment in the first five words. |
| **Confusion** | Two ideas in one line, passive voice, a term the viewer doesn't know ("reciprocity bias"). | One idea, active voice, plain words. Read it once at speed: one meaning only. |
| **Irrelevance** | "Business" or "marketing" in general, "people", "anyone". | A specific moment (the quote, the call, the quiet client, the event) or a specific brand. |
| **Disinterest** | Clear and relevant, but no pull. | Add the A-vs-B contrast or a loop line. |
| **Label, not lead** | It says what the video is about but goes nowhere ("Here's how Apple sells laptops."). | Drop the viewer in and point at the payoff. |
| **Citation opener** | "In 2011, researchers at Oxford…" | Start with what happened, as a scene. The source goes in the fact sheet. |

## Visual hook (frame one)

The first frame must show the situation the first line names: the product on the shelf, the phone with the unanswered quote, the client on the call, the crowded event. The on-screen text hook is a **shortened** version of line 1, never a different idea. See `12-visual-storyboard.md`.

## Title / cover text
The on-screen title and the cover title are a short version of the hook, in sentence case, 4 to 9 words. (The caption is different: a teaser that names nothing from the video. `05-endings-and-ctas.md`.) Patterns that already work in the niche:
- **Why [brand] [does the odd thing].** ("Why Costco's chicken is at the back")
- **[Brand] [surprising fact].** ("Häagen-Dazs isn't from Denmark")
- **Save this before [your next sales call / you send that quote / your next event].**
- **[Thing you say] is why nobody remembers you.**
- **Talk to this person first.**
- **The [one/first] [line/question/text] that [outcome].**
Never vague ("Business tips"), never all caps clickbait the video can't pay off.
