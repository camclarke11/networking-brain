## What the data says (and how to act on it)

### 1. The audience and the situation matter more than anything else
@theoryofbehaviour posted five general psychology reels first (hedonic adaptation, the halo effect, persuasion): **median 447 views**. From 10 October it narrowed to networking for young guys starting a business: **median 19,050 views**, then **688k and 527k** on the last two posts.

**Do:** every script is set in one specific situation a young founder actually faces. At an event, in a DM, in a coffee meeting, being introduced, following up.
**Don't:** explain a psychology concept in the abstract. If the viewer can't picture where they'd use it this week, it's Tier 3.

### 2. Topic tiers (use these on every idea)

| Tier | What it is | Reference median | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | A live networking moment with exact words or one clear move: events, connectors, outreach DMs, reading whether someone wants to keep talking | ~22k (7 posts, both breakouts) | FORD questions, find the connector, coffee DM, Question Back test, ask for a small favour |
| **Tier 2** | Adjacent skills framed for founders: how you describe yourself, positioning, confidence in a room, making someone comfortable | ~3.2k (3 posts) | The intro line, your label, three steps to make anyone comfortable |
| **Tier 3** | General psychology or persuasion with no specific networking situation | ~430 (4 posts) | Hedonic adaptation, halo effect, 300 pennies, the loudest person in the room |

Tier 3 ideas can be rescued by moving them into a Tier 1 situation. The halo effect is Tier 3. "The first 30 seconds at an event decide how they rate everything else you say" is Tier 1.

### 3. The two breakouts share a shape
Both are about **one in-person event moment**, both open on a line the viewer has said or done themselves (N1, N2), both give **one concrete move** (a person to find, four questions to ask), both **voice the viewer's objection**, and both end on a **share prompt** aimed at a specific friend. Neither cites a study. See `09-annotated-breakdowns.md`.

### 4. Proof is optional, specifics are not
Study citations appear across the whole range, from 447 to 32k views, and neither breakout uses one. Posts with no proof at all have the highest median (about 22k across 5 posts) because they include both breakouts. A study helps when it makes the move feel safe to try ("A 2015 study found people who ask for advice look more competent"). It never carries a video on its own.

### 5. Saying the objection out loud is the most consistent beat
The viewer's doubt is voiced in 9 of the 14 transcripts, including both breakouts ("You're thinking, why would a guy like that help me?"). Those posts have a higher median (about 5.2k against 3.2k) and hold 98% of the account's views. Keep it in every script unless there is genuinely no objection.

### 5b. End on a share prompt aimed at one specific friend
The five posts ending "Send this to the friend who…" have a median of about 22k, and include both breakouts. Shares are how a 0-follower account reaches new people. Default to it.

### 6. The caption does a different job
Their videos are short (about 145 to 155 words for the winners) and the caption holds the full breakdown: lists, exact lines, a challenge. That is what makes people save. Every script we write ships with a saveable caption (`05-endings-and-ctas.md`).

## Caveats (don't over-fit)
- The two breakouts are the newest posts. Account momentum and the algorithm testing a new niche are part of the story, not just the script.
- One export means newer posts have had less time to collect views, which **understates** the latest posts. The real gap is probably bigger.
- Views only. We have no retention or watch-time data for this account, so we can't see where people swipe away.

## Label taxonomy (for `data/<handle>/post-tags.csv`)

Every post needs one row. The numbers are automatic; these labels are judgement calls, so use these exact values.

| Column | Values |
|---|---|
| `category` | `event_conversation` (what to say or do at an event), `outreach` (DMs, cold asks, favours, coffee requests), `positioning` (how you describe yourself, labels, intro lines), `reading_people` (signals, interest, when to leave), `follow_up` (after the meeting or intro), `confidence`, `persuasion`, `general_psych` |
| `tier` | `1`, `2`, `3` (section 2 above) |
| `format` | `F1`–`F6` from `06-formats.md` |
| `hook` | `N1`–`N8` from `03-hooks.md`, or `other` |
| `named_tool` | `yes` if the script coins or uses a named tool, test or acronym (FORD, the Question Back test), else `no` |
| `proof` | `study`, `history` (a historical story), `both`, `concept` (names a bias with no study), `none` |
| `objection` | `yes` if the script voices the viewer's doubt ("You're thinking…"), else `no` |
| `character` | `named` ("Say his name's Daniel"), `historic` (a real historical figure), `generic` ("two guys", "the guy who built the agency"), `none` |
| `ending` | `E1`–`E5` from `05-endings-and-ctas.md` |
| `cta` | `share`, `comment`, `follow`, `caption`, `none`, `unknown` |
| `reupload_of` | the original post's ID if this is a re-upload, else blank |
| `notes` | one line: the tool, the study, anything unusual |

## Keeping this file current
1. New export → `python3 tools/performance.py import "<export folder>"`.
2. `python3 tools/performance.py tag` → label every new post using the taxonomy above.
3. `python3 tools/performance.py report` → regenerates the live tables below.
4. Compare the new tables with "What the data says". If a pattern moved, rewrite that section and note the date.
