# 00 — What Actually Performs (read this first)

> **Overrides everything else.** When this file and any other knowledge file disagree, this file wins. When the live tables at the bottom disagree with the written rules, the live tables win and the rules get rewritten.

## How this file works

- The top half is written by hand: what the numbers mean and what to do about them.
- The bottom half is generated by `python3 tools/performance.py report`, one section per account (`## Live data: @handle`). Never edit inside the `AUTO:START` / `AUTO:END` markers.
- Data so far: the networking competitor **@theoryofbehaviour** (14 posts, exported 2026-10-20), the Brand X-ray reference **@decoyreport** (23 posts, 2026-10-21), Elliott's **@zlliott** (36 posts, 2026-09-28, for *how* stories are told; §3b) and **our own retention tests** (§4). Once our own account is live, its data goes in its own section and **our numbers beat theirs** whenever the two disagree.
- Small numbers. Call a pattern "strong" only with 3+ posts behind it, and treat everything as correlation.

## What the data says (and how to act on it)

**The niche (from 2026-10-22):** making your business better, two pillars (the Brand X-ray and the Move), feeding a product, the Playbook (`01-audience-and-account.md`). The data below comes from three reference accounts: @theoryofbehaviour shows what works for *moves* (its niche, networking for young founders, is now one chapter of ours), @decoyreport shows what works for *brand tricks* (§7), and @zlliott shows how a script should be *told* (§3b). The playbook-style account the owner found isn't imported yet (§8).

### 1. The audience and the situation matter more than anything else
@theoryofbehaviour posted five general psychology reels first (hedonic adaptation, the halo effect, persuasion): **median 447 views**. From 10 October it narrowed to networking for young guys starting a business: **median 19,050 views**, then **688k and 527k** on the last two posts. @decoyreport shows the same thing for brands: a famous brand plus a trick you can use in your own business got a 115k median; brand history with no trick to steal got 27k; shopper-side advice got 16k.

**Do:** every script hands a young founder one thing to use in their own business this week, set in one specific moment (a sales call, quoting a price, a client going quiet, a room full of strangers) or stolen from one famous brand.
**Don't:** explain a psychology concept, a business theory or a brand's history in the abstract. If the viewer can't picture using it in their business this week, it's Tier 3.

### 2. Topic tiers (use these on every idea)

| Tier | What it is | Reference median | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | One business moment with exact words or one clear move (a sales call, a price, a follow-up, an intro, a client update), or a famous brand's trick with a rule to steal | ~22k (@theoryofbehaviour, 7 posts, both breakouts); ~115k (@decoyreport brand + trick, 13 posts) | Find the connector, the coffee DM, Apple's tilted laptops, the price with no comma |
| **Tier 2** | Adjacent and useful but not one moment: positioning, how you describe yourself, confidence, a brand's history with a loose lesson | ~3.2k (@theoryofbehaviour, 3 posts); ~27k (@decoyreport history, 7 posts) | Your label, the intro line, LEGO's comeback |
| **Tier 3** | General psychology, mindset, motivation or business theory with no moment and no move | ~430 (@theoryofbehaviour, 4 posts) | Hedonic adaptation, the halo effect, "the loudest person in the room" |

Tier 3 ideas can be rescued by moving them into a Tier 1 moment. "The halo effect" is Tier 3. "The first 30 seconds of a sales call decide how they rate everything else you say" is Tier 1.

### 3. The two breakouts share a shape
Both are about **one in-person event moment**, both open on a line the viewer has said or done themselves (N1, N2), both give **one concrete move** (a person to find, four questions to ask), both **voice the viewer's objection**, and both end on a **share prompt** aimed at a specific friend. Neither cites a study. See `09-annotated-breakdowns.md`.

### 3b. The hook leads, and every line after it keeps leading (added 2026-10-21)
Elliott's account (@zlliott, 36 posts, export of 28 September 2026) is the cleanest test we have of *how* a script is told:

| Shape | Posts | Median views | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Second-person story: "You're standing inside a post office holding a shotgun." Present tense, the viewer in the scene from word one | 23 | **30.9k** | 1.8k to **775k** (all of his top 12) |
| Other lessons (lists, first person, "have you ever noticed") | 10 | 5.9k | 2.6k to 27k |
| Lessons that explain with "researchers found", "psychologists call it", "neuroscientists call this" | 3 | **3.2k** | 2.2k to 3.5k |

His winners do the same five things: line 1 drops "you" into a moment you can see; the hook points at something that hasn't happened yet ("and the crime that eventually puts you away for life hasn't even happened"); every few lines a BUT flips what you expected; the stakes rise every beat; the last line calls back to the first (the £26 robbery → "He's still inside"). The rules live in `04-lesson-engine.md`.
Caveats: the shape is necessary, not sufficient. His D.B. Cooper story ran twice with almost the same words and got 115k and 13k, and the JFK heist (same shape) got 5k.

### 4. Never cite in the script. Specifics are the proof (rewritten 2026-10-21)
**Our own test videos lost retention on the lines that narrated research** ("In 2009, researchers… found"). Every other account agrees:
- Elliott told the Malone Lam story three times. The two versions that just tell it got **662k** and **164k**. The version that stops to explain ("Psychologists call it the hot-cold empathy gap…") and adds a moral got **51k**.
- His three science-label lessons are his weakest group (table above).
- Neither @theoryofbehaviour breakout cites a study, and its no-proof posts have the highest median (about 22k across 5 posts). Study citations appear across its whole range, from 447 to 32k.

**Do:** verify every fact this session and keep the source in the fact sheet. In the script, tell the fact as *what happened*, ideally to "you" inside a scene ("You're handed the same yogurt in three bowls. The heavy one tastes richer."). Exact numbers, times and places convince better than a name with a title.
**Don't:** "researchers found", "a study", "studies show", "scientists", "psychologists call it", a named effect, or anyone's credentials in the spoken script. `tools/check_script.py` fails them. Credit the source in the caption if you want to.
(@decoyreport's two study-plus-brand posts did well, 1.35M median. We keep their *facts* and drop the citation, because our own retention says so.)

### 5. Saying the objection out loud is the most consistent beat
The viewer's doubt is voiced in 9 of the 14 transcripts, including both breakouts ("You're thinking, why would a guy like that help me?"). Those posts have a higher median (about 5.2k against 3.2k) and hold 98% of the account's views. Keep it in every script unless there is genuinely no objection.

### 5b. End on a share prompt aimed at one specific friend
The five posts ending "Send this to the friend who…" have a median of about 22k, and include both breakouts. Shares are how a 0-follower account reaches new people. Default to it while the Playbook is coming soon (no product CTA in the video yet). When it goes live, the Playbook line joins the ending (`05-endings-and-ctas.md`); test share-then-CTA against CTA-only and let our own data decide.

### 6. The caption does a different job
Their videos are short (about 145 to 155 words for the winners) and the caption holds the full breakdown: lists, exact lines, a challenge. That is what makes people save. **We chose differently on 2026-10-22:** our captions are *teasers*: one question that doesn't even say what the video is about, so the caption sends people into the video, and the full breakdown is saved for the Playbook (`05-endings-and-ctas.md`). This is a bet on watch time over saves. Check it against our own data (saves, watch time, sends) after the first two weeks.

### 7. The Brand X-ray pillar (@decoyreport, added 2026-10-21)
A famous brand doing something odd on purpose, the mechanism, then **"So if you sell anything…"**: median 115k across 13 posts, a 9.75M breakout and a 2.6M. Brand history with no trick to steal: 27k. Full read in `15-brand-xray.md`. Tiers for this pillar: **Tier 1** = a known brand plus a trick a one-person business can use this week; **Tier 2** = a brand lesson with no concrete move; **Tier 3** = shopper-side advice.

### 8. The playbook account (pending, added 2026-10-22)
The owner found an account in this exact niche (teaching people how to make their business better, with the CTA "the playbook's in my bio"). It isn't in the repo yet. When its Media Tools export arrives: `python3 tools/performance.py import "<export folder>"`, tag it, report it, add it to `reference_accounts` in `config.json`, and write its findings here. It will tell us which chapters and hooks sell the Playbook best, and how its CTA affects views.

## Caveats (don't over-fit)
- The two breakouts are the newest posts. Account momentum and the algorithm testing a new niche are part of the story, not just the script.
- One export means newer posts have had less time to collect views, which **understates** the latest posts. The real gap is probably bigger.
- Views only. We have no retention or watch-time data for this account, so we can't see where people swipe away.

## Label taxonomy (for `data/<handle>/post-tags.csv`)

Every post needs one row. The numbers are automatic; these labels are judgement calls, so use these exact values.

| Column | Values |
|---|---|
| `category` | Networking: `event_conversation` (what to say or do at an event), `outreach` (DMs, cold asks, favours, coffee requests), `positioning` (how you describe yourself, labels, intro lines), `reading_people` (signals, interest, when to leave), `follow_up` (after the meeting or intro), `confidence`, `persuasion`, `general_psych`. Brand X-ray (F7): `pricing`, `retail`, `packaging`, `brand_strategy`, `consumer_psych` |
| `tier` | `1`, `2`, `3` (section 2 above) |
| `format` | `F1`–`F7` from `06-formats.md` |
| `hook` | `N1`–`N11` from `03-hooks.md`, or `other` |
| `named_tool` | `yes` if the script coins or uses a named tool, test or acronym (FORD, the Question Back test), else `no` |
| `proof` | `study`, `history` (a historical story), `both`, `concept` (names a bias with no study), `none` |
| `objection` | `yes` if the script voices the viewer's doubt ("You're thinking…"), else `no` |
| `character` | `named` ("Say his name's Daniel"), `historic` (a real historical figure), `generic` ("two guys", "the guy who built the agency"), `none` |
| `ending` | `E1`–`E6` from `05-endings-and-ctas.md` |
| `cta` | `share`, `comment`, `follow`, `caption`, `none`, `unknown` |
| `reupload_of` | the original post's ID if this is a re-upload, else blank |
| `notes` | one line: the tool, the study, anything unusual |

## Keeping this file current
1. New export → `python3 tools/performance.py import "<export folder>"`.
2. `python3 tools/performance.py tag` → label every new post using the taxonomy above.
3. `python3 tools/performance.py report` → regenerates the live tables below.
4. Compare the new tables with "What the data says". If a pattern moved, rewrite that section and note the date.
