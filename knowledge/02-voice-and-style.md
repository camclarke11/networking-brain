# 02 — Voice and Style

The narrator is **the older brother who has already done it**. A couple of years ahead: landed the first clients, set the wrong prices, been ghosted, studied how the big brands do it, and now tells you the one move that works. Calm, direct, a bit amused at how simple it is. Never a guru, never a hype man, never a lecturer.

---

## 1. Point of view

- **"You" is the viewer, and the viewer is in the scene.** "You finally go to the event." "You DM him." The viewer is the main character, in present tense.
- **"I" is the narrator**, used sparingly and only to:
  - coin a tool: "I call it the Question Back test."
  - admit a mistake: "Mine was 'so… do you come here often'."
  - Use "I" at most 2 times per script.
- **Other people are third person, and often named:** "Say his name's Daniel." A name makes a person easy to picture and gives the 3D cast someone to play.
- Density: "you" about **7 to 10 times per 100 words**. Lower than that and the script has drifted into a lecture.

## 2. Rhythm

- **One sentence per line.** Long thoughts split at a breath.
- **Jagged lengths:** fragments ("You don't." "Tonight, that's you.") next to 15 to 20 word lines. Never three lines in a row of the same length.
- **Short is the default.** The reference account's winners average about 7 words a line.
- **Paired contrasts** carry the big ideas: "A job title ends a conversation. A project starts one." "People forget what you do. They remember what you noticed about them."
- **Question then answer:** "So why would a guy like that help you? Because introducing people is his favorite thing."

## 3. Words

- **Plain, spoken, about a 5th-grade reading level** (`16-short-form-guide.md` B1). "Talk to" not "engage with". "Remember you" not "retain a positive impression".
- **Spelling:** set in `config.json` (`spelling`). Default **US English** ("favor", "realize") because the audience is global and the reference account uses it. Money in dollars.
- **Contractions always:** you're, don't, he'll, that's.
- **The niche's own nouns:** client, customer, offer, price, quote, invoice, proposal, call, DM, follow-up, referral, intro, launch, brand, shop, agency, founder, "your business". Use them; they signal "this is for you". Avoid corporate words (stakeholder, synergy, value proposition, KPI, ROI).
- **Swearing:** none. The account should be safe to share with a mate, a boss or a mum.

### Banned (instant AI voice, or instant guru voice)
delve, tapestry, testament, unravel, navigate, realm, embark, intricate, pivotal, leverage, synergy, unlock, game-changer, level up, "in today's world", "in a world where", "let's dive in", "buckle up", "here's the kicker", "the secret nobody tells you", "this will change your life", "hack" (as a noun for a tip), "alpha", "high-value man", "manipulate", "dark psychology". No em-dashes or semicolons in the script body. No rhetorical triple questions.

## 4. Specifics

Specifics make advice feel tested instead of made up.
- **Exact words in quotes:** the opener, the DM, the follow-up text, word for word. "You did X. I'm about to do the same. If you were me, A or B?" is worth ten lines of theory.
- **Exact numbers:** "three people this week", "10 seconds", "four letters".
- **Exact people:** "the guy who built the agency you want", not "a successful person".
- **Facts, told not cited.** Say what happened, not who found it. Not "In 2011, researchers at Oxford gave people yogurt in three bowls." Instead: "You're handed the same yogurt in three bowls. The heaviest one tastes richer." No "researchers", "a study", "studies show", "scientists", "psychologists call it" or named effects in the script (`00-what-performs.md` §4). The source lives in the fact sheet.

## 5. Dialogue

The lines the viewer will actually say are the product. Write them so they sound natural out loud, not clever on paper. Read each one as if you're saying it to a stranger holding a drink. If you'd cringe, rewrite it.

## 6. Formatting the spoken script

- Only words the narrator says out loud. No headings, brackets, emojis or stage directions.
- Dialogue the viewer says goes inline without quote marks if it reads naturally aloud ("You ask, are you from here originally?"). The storyboard handles what appears on screen.
- Numbers as they're spoken: "three minutes", "1969", "$50 a month".
